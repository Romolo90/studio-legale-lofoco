Italian film and audiovisual tax credit

We assist producers, authors and companies throughout the tax credit process: from checking the requirements to structuring the contracts behind the work, from filing applications on DGCOL to the reporting stage, and through to litigation when the benefit is refused or revoked.

A fiscal benefit decided on legal grounds

The Italian film and audiovisual tax credit is governed by Law no. 220 of 14 November 2016 and its implementing ministerial decrees, which have been reformed and subsequently amended in recent years. It is a generous instrument, but it works less automatically than the name suggests: the amount follows from the figures, while entitlement to it turns on legal questions decided much earlier.

In our experience, those who lose the benefit rarely get the arithmetic wrong. The problem lies upstream: a co-production agreement that does not hold up against the access requirements, qualified expenditure that is poorly documented, an application filed too late in relation to the start of production, a combination with other measures that was never verified. Each of these costs little to address at the outset and a great deal to repair at the end.

The lines we cover

The Directorate-General for Cinema and Audiovisual divides the tax credit into distinct lines, each with its own requirements, rates and notices. We work in particular on:

  • National production — film, television and web works of Italian nationality
  • Production of foreign works — foreign productions shot in Italy, covered in our guide to the tax credit for foreign productions
  • Production with external investors — contributions from outside the industry
  • Development — the stage preceding production itself
  • National and international distribution
  • Technical industries and post-production
  • Exhibition and cinema upgrading
  • Videogame production
  • Music

Rates, expenditure thresholds and filing windows differ from line to line and are updated at each session, which is why we do not reproduce them here. They are set out in the official notices, which we follow and comment on in our Insights.

Where we come in

Access requirements

Italian nationality of the work and cultural eligibility are two separate procedures, each with its own documentation. We prepare and review the supporting material for both, including the technical and artistic reports for the points-based test.

Contractual structure of the work

Production and co-production agreements, chain of title, relations with authors and screenwriters, agreements with distributors and platforms. This is where it is settled whether the requirements will be met: stepping in after signature almost always means renegotiating.

DGCOL applications and dealings with the administration

Preliminary and final applications, handling requests for further documentation, correspondence with the Directorate-General. Timing is decisive here and missed deadlines cannot be recovered.

Qualified expenditure, reporting and combination of measures

Qualification of expenditure incurred in Italy, consistency between the declared budget and the final account, advance verification of how the credit combines with selective and automatic contributions and regional incentives. A badly structured combination surfaces once the money has already been spent.

Refusals, revocations and litigation

Observations within the administrative procedure, applications for review and challenges before the administrative court. Time limits are short and run from notification of the decision, so the assessment has to be made straight away.

Italian nationality connects the work to the Italian legal system on the basis of production criteria and crew composition; cultural eligibility is a points-based assessment of the content and cultural elements of the work. A project may satisfy one and not the other, and each must be documented with its own material.

Deadlines differ between lines and change from one session to the next: they must be checked against the notice in force before the production schedule is set, not afterwards.

Combination is possible within precise limits, which depend on the nature of the resources, the share of the cost of the work being covered and the rules of each individual measure. This is one of the areas where mistakes are most frequent and most expensive, because they surface at the reporting stage, once the expenditure has already been incurred.

Depending on the stage, it is possible to respond to a request for further documentation, file observations within the administrative procedure, or challenge the decision before the administrative court within the applicable time limits, which are short. Since those limits run from notification of the decision, the measure should be examined immediately.

The two roles are complementary. The accountant handles quantification of the credit, its use as a set-off and the tax aspects. The lawyer addresses the conditions on which entitlement depends: contractual structure, ownership of rights, the co-production arrangement, access requirements and dealings with the administration, including any litigation.
