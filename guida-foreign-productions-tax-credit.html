Italian tax credit for foreign productions

The Italian tax credit for foreign productions is not paid to the foreign producer. It goes to the Italian production services company (impresa di produzione esecutiva) or post-production company that carries out the work in Italy on commission. It also explains a restriction on rights to be checked before the service agreement is signed.

What the credit is, and who receives it

The credit is granted to Italian production services (executive production) and post-production companies, on the expenditure they incur on Italian territory to make an audiovisual work that does not hold Italian nationality, using Italian or European Economic Area labour, on commission from foreign production companies. The foreign producer is the client, not the beneficiary. (art. 2(1), Interministerial Decree 329/2024)

Article 19 of the film and audiovisual law sets a band rather than a figure, a credit of not less than 25 and not more than 40 per cent of the expenditure incurred in Italy. The implementing decree sits at the top of that band. (Law 220/2016, art. 19; Decree 329/2024)

The work must also meet the cultural eligibility requirements set out in Table A to the decree, and works falling within the exclusions set by the decree adopted under article 14(2) of the law are not admissible in any event. (art. 2(4) and (5))

How much it is worth

The credit is 40 per cent of the eligible production cost of the single work, as defined in Table B annexed to the decree. (art. 3(1))

Above-the-line costs attributable to individuals not tax resident in a European Economic Area country attract a 30 per cent rate instead. It is a lower rate, not a cap. (art. 3(2))

The benefit applies only if the eligible cost is at least 250,000 euro, and it is capped at 20 million euro per year for each company or group of companies. The credit taken by the same company for the production of works of Italian nationality does not count towards that annual cap. The total production cost of the work must be attested by the foreign commissioning company. (art. 3(3), (4) and (5))

The credit and any other form of public support may not together exceed 50 per cent of the cost of the work. Regional funds and film commission grants therefore reduce the headroom. (art. 5(5))

Eligible cost is not the cost of the film

Above-the-line costs, meaning story and screenplay, direction and principal cast, are eligible up to 30 per cent of the total production cost. Costs incurred in Italy for the use of artificial intelligence that relate to the creative or artistic services within those items are not eligible. The single exception is artificial intelligence used for special effects under the principal cast item. (art. 4(2)(b))

Financial, insurance and guarantee charges are eligible up to 7.5 per cent of the total production cost, and only if directly and exclusively attributable to the specific work. The producer fee and the company's general overheads are not eligible, and each may be booked in the total production cost up to 7.5 per cent of it. (art. 4(2)(a) and (c))

Costs of personnel covered by national collective agreements are eligible, for each worker, within the amount set by those agreements, which may be increased by up to 20 per cent. (art. 4(2)(d))

Who is paid also matters. Expenditure qualifies where it is paid to parties tax resident in Italy, to companies taxed in Italy and to non-resident individuals taxed in Italy on the specific income and, on a reciprocity basis, to companies of another European Economic Area country with a branch, agency or office in Italy that operates mainly there and is taxed there, together with the costs of the industry's COVID-19 safety protocol and of environmental sustainability protocols. (art. 4(3))

Where part of the work is contracted out under a service agreement, those costs are eligible only if the contractor has its registered office in Italy and does not sub-contract onward down a chain. With the final application the beneficiary itemises each service with its cost and the personnel involved, and attaches a self-declaration by the supplier that there is no chain sub-contracting and that the expenditure is eligible. (Decree 329/2024; Directorial Decree 2540/2025)

The implementing directorial decree adds payment rules, on pain of the cost becoming ineligible. Invoices, expense documents and payments above 1,000 euro must state the title of the work, payments must be traceable and go through the dedicated bank account, and costs settled by offsetting debts and credits with suppliers are not eligible. (art. 5(2)(b), Directorial Decree 2540/2025)

Expenditure used for other States' tax credits is not eligible. (art. 4(5))

Requirements, and the one to check first

The applicant must have its registered office in the European Economic Area and be taxed in Italy, by residence or through a permanent establishment to which the work is attributable, be a limited company (società di capitali) with fully paid-up share capital and net assets of at least 40,000 euro, 10,000 euro for short films, not be a non-profit association or foundation, and hold ATECO code J 59.11. It must comply with social security, tax, insurance and workplace safety rules and collective agreements, must not be barred from public contracts or in insolvency liquidation proceedings, and must follow the industry protocol against harassment and violence. (art. 2(3))

One requirement bears directly on the service agreement with the commissioning company. The Italian company must not hold any share of the rights in the work. A derogation is possible, but only on express decision of the Directorate General and only where it is shown that the work objectively could not be made through an international co-production, co-participation or international production agreement. (art. 3(6))

The cost certification filed with the final application must expressly attest compliance with it, alongside the effectiveness of the expenditure and its strict attribution to the work. The certification is signed digitally, is paid for by the applicant, and a certifier issuing an inaccurate certification faces an administrative fine of 10,000 to 50,000 euro for each one. (art. 5(1) and (3))

Windows and deadlines

Each annual session is opened by a directorial decree. The 2026 session ran from 12.00 on 3 June 2026 to 23.59 on 31 August 2026 and is now closed. (art. 2(1), Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

The preliminary application must be filed, on pain of inadmissibility, «non oltre novanta giorni prima della data di inizio delle fasi di lavorazione» (literally, no later than ninety days before the start of work) and includes the contract with the foreign production and the commissioning company's statement of total cost. Read literally, it sets a minimum lead time, but the implementing directorial decree requires proof that work has started and run for at least four weeks, or 50 per cent of working days, with the application or within 120 days of filing, which sits uneasily with filing more than ninety days ahead. The texts do not settle the point, and the Directorate General's practice should be checked before fixing the filing date. The final application is due within 180 days after the end of the work, and only for this deadline may derogations be allowed, for supervening reasons promptly notified and documented. It may also be filed alone. (Decree 329/2024; Directorial Decree 2540/2025)

A processing fee is payable on pain of inadmissibility, set by decree between 200 and 10,000 euro. For this line it is 5,000 euro where the cost borne by the executive production or post-production company is 3.5 million euro or less, and 10,000 euro above that threshold. The basis is the Italian company's own cost, not the total cost of the work, and if the cost stated in the final application moves the work into the higher bracket the difference must be paid when that application is filed. (Table 1.A and art. 1(3) and (4), Directorial Decree 3362/2024 as amended, consolidated text)

In the 2026 session applications are examined in order of the declared start date of the work, and only at equal start dates in order of filing, and credits are recognised only within the budget allocated. Within 60 days of the close of the window the Directorate General notifies applicants meeting the requirements of the credit due. Eligible applications left unfunded are carried over automatically, unless the applicant indicates otherwise and with no further fee, to any later session, which in 2026 may open only if the budget is increased. (arts. 1(2) and 4, Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

The annual decree also adds further grounds of inadmissibility beyond the principal decree. The application must be accompanied by an insurance contract covering damage to the assets listed in article 2424 of the Civil Code, balance-sheet item B-II, numbers 1), 2) and 3), directly caused by natural disasters and catastrophic events, it must be signed by the legal representative with a digital or other qualified electronic signature, and the work must not meet, even potentially, the requirements for recognition of Italian nationality, which tightens a condition already found in the principal decree. (art. 5, Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

After approval, and how the credit is lost

The credit is recognised once the review has a positive outcome, within the available budget, and may be used to offset tax at 70 per cent upon approval of the preliminary application and the remainder upon approval of the final one. The 70 per cent is reduced to 40 per cent for companies that specifically ask for it. Where only the final application is filed, the whole approved amount may be used at once. Offsetting is available from the fifteenth day of the month following recognition, through the Revenue Agency's online services. (Decree 329/2024; Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

If eligible costs in the final application exceed the preliminary figure by more than 10 per cent, the credit is calculated on that figure plus at most 10 per cent. Derogations exist for proven substantial changes in the production structure and for force majeure arising from unforeseeable events of a general nature unconnected with the specific production. The derogation for substantial changes must be requested together with the final application, and both remain subject to prior verification that funds are available. (art. 8(6) and (7))

Several obligations carry forfeiture of the benefit. Where there are concrete risk conditions, the work must carry the insurance cover listed in the decree, distinct from the natural disaster policy of the annual decree. With the final application the beneficiary discloses which stages of the work carried out in Italy used artificial intelligence, and the producer reports to the Directorate General the data in its possession, including on commercial exploitation. Contracts with authors, performers and executants must allow them, on pain of inadmissibility, to withhold consent to the exploitation of their work, image or performance by artificial intelligence systems. Contracts must finally provide that the credits and promotional materials carry the Ministry of Culture's name and logo and a statement that the work was made with the support of the Fund for the development of investment in cinema and audiovisual, with the same prominence as the producer's own. (art. 9)

The credit lapses if cultural eligibility is refused or falls away, or if any other requirement of the decree is not met, and any benefit already used is recovered with interest and penalties. (art. 10)

The credit approved on a final basis may be assigned to banking, financial and insurance intermediaries subject to prudential supervision, who may use it only to offset their own tax or social security debts. The assignment requires an application to the Directorate General accompanied by proof of payment of the processing fee, and an assignment contract in public form. The Directorate verifies the credit within 60 days, and the assignee uses it from the tenth day of the month after acceptance. The assignment cannot be authorised before the Directorate has received the link to view the work, and the Directorate may request an appraisal of the costs for this purpose too. (Decree 329/2024; Directorial Decree 2540/2025)
