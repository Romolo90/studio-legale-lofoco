Tax Credit Production 2026 Application Checklist

Practical tool by Studio Legale Lo Foco (Rome). Does not replace official assessment, cultural eligibility test or DGCOL submission. Always verify the latest DG Cinema notices.

Updated 29 September 2026. Legal basis: art. 15 of Law no. 220 of 14 November 2016 and Interministerial Decree MiC-MEF of 10 July 2024, rep. no. 225, as amended by Interministerial Decree of 22 April 2025, rep. no. 141 and by Interministerial Decree of 6 July 2026, rep. no. 281, which lowered rates and caps with no transitional provisions.
Rates. Film works: 30% of the eligible cost for independent producers (art. 13(1)); for European independent undertakings with high production and financial capacity, 25% on the portion of eligible cost above €5m and up to €10m, and 20% above €10m (art. 13(2)); 20% for non-independent producers and non-European undertakings, up to €3m per year per undertaking or group (art. 13(3)). Television and web works: 20%, raised to 30% in the cases listed in art. 17(1). Documentaries 30% (art. 21), animation 35% (art. 24), short films 30% (art. 28), music videos 30%, with the credit capped at €80,000 per work (art. 30).
Caps. The credit cannot exceed €4m per film work (including theatrical documentaries and animation) and €7m per television or web work, with annual caps per undertaking of €7m and €15m. Where at least 40% of the total production cost is covered by resources from outside Italy the maximum rises to €6m and €10m (art. 5). Total public support stays within 50% of the cost of the work, subject to the exceptions in art. 4.
2026 session. Applications on DGCOL from 10:00 a.m. on 30 September to 11:59 p.m. on 14 October 2026. They are processed by the start date of shooting (for animation, of production work) until resources run out, with no partial credit; eligible applications left out for lack of funds may be refiled in the next session (art. 6-bis; Directorial Decree of 24 September 2026, rep. no. 2821, arts. 2 and 3).

1. Preliminary requirements

  • Italian nationality, including for co-productions, and the cultural eligibility requirements of Table A (minimum score 50/100), both of which are needed (art. 2(5))
  • Applicant requirements: limited company with fully paid-up share capital and net equity of at least €40,000 (€10,000 for short films), registered office in the European Economic Area, taxation in Italy, ATECO code J 59.11, not required for music videos (art. 2(4))
  • Fiction film works longer than 52 minutes: financing covering at least 40% of the production cost, excluding the requested tax credit, and a binding agreement with a theatrical distributor, including a promotion plan and minimum numbers of screenings and cinemas, checked with the final application (art. 12(1)). Decree 141/2025 removed the requirement that the financing come from private sources. The consolidated text published by the Directorate-General reads 30%, but neither Decree 141/2025 nor Decree 281/2026 changed the 40% threshold, so document the higher one. Alternatively, works awarded a selective grant under calls published after 1 January 2024 or funding from supranational bodies under Council of Europe or European Union programmes, subject to the minimum theatrical release requirement (art. 12(2))
  • Television and web works: they must be fiction, with a total running time of at least 52 minutes and a total cost of at least €2,000 per minute, save for derogation by the Directorate-General (art. 16(6)); they require an original independent producer, financing covering at least 50% and an agreement with a national broadcaster or an SVOD service meeting the decree's requirements, which takes part in the work or pays at least 20% of its cost (art. 16(1) and (2)). For works with a selective grant or supranational funding the financing and 20% thresholds do not apply, and the agreements are filed by the final application (art. 16(3))
  • Documentaries, animation and short films: financing covering at least 30% and the distribution agreements required for each type (arts. 20, 23 and 27); for short films an eligible cost of no more than €200,000 (art. 27(1)). Music videos: financing covering at least 40% (art. 30(1))
  • Preliminary application filed after the request for provisional Italian nationality and no earlier than sixty days before the start of four consecutive weeks of shooting or of 50% of shooting days (arts. 14 and 18). For documentaries and short films the reference is the start of 50% of shooting days (arts. 22 and 29), for animation the start of 50% of production days or of four consecutive weeks of production (art. 25)
  • Natural-catastrophe insurance policy (art. 1(101)-(102), Law 213/2023), without which the application is inadmissible (art. 7(11))
  • Contracts with authors and performers containing clauses that allow them to refuse the use of their work or performance by artificial intelligence systems, and contracts with media service providers and distributors requiring transparency on audience data, on pain of inadmissibility (art. 7(6)) and, for the audience-data clause, of inadmissibility or forfeiture (art. 7(2))
  • Work produced, distributed and made available so that persons with disabilities can access it, including through subtitles and audio description, on pain of inadmissibility or forfeiture (art. 7(4))
  • Where there are several producers, a joint application (art. 14(3))
  • Application signed with the digital signature of the legal representative (Directorial Decree 2821/2026, art. 4)

2. Documents to prepare (core list)

  • Application on the Directorate-General's forms, completed on DGCOL and digitally signed
  • Production / development contract + any co-production agreements
  • Detailed budget showing total cost and eligible cost under art. 3 and Table B
  • Production schedule / plan (timeline, locations, key cast & crew)
  • Cultural eligibility documentation under Table A of Decree 225/2024
  • IP / rights documentation (author contracts, screenplay, treatment)
  • Company certificates and extracts
  • Proof of payment of the processing fee, between €200 and €10,000, without which the application is inadmissible (art. 2(3))
  • Contract with the authors of story and screenplay, with receipt of payment and a substitute declaration under art. 47 of Presidential Decree 445/2000 that no side agreements alter its economic terms (art. 14(2))
  • Evidence of the minimum financing (Directorial Decree 2821/2026, art. 4) and of the declared start date of shooting, which the Directorate-General may check (Directorial Decree 2821/2026, art. 5)
  • Distribution or broadcasting agreements required for the type of work; for television and web works they must already be documented in the preliminary application (art. 18(2)), except for works with a selective grant or supranational funding (art. 16(3))
  • For "difficult works" (art. 4(3)), evidence of the requirements, relevant to the 80% aid-intensity ceiling

3. Recommended operational steps

  1. Preliminary feasibility check, also with the firm's Incentives Checker
  2. Prepare the budget separating total cost and eligible cost; only expenditure paid to suppliers resident or taxable in Italy is eligible, under the conditions of art. 3(3), save for the share of European Economic Area expenditure allowed for documentaries by art. 21(1)
  3. Prepare the cultural eligibility documentation (Table A)
  4. Submit the preliminary application and attachments to DGCOL within the session window, observing the sixty-day rule; the start date of shooting, or of production work for animation, sets the processing order
  5. Monitor platform communications and requests for additional documents
  6. Retain all expenditure evidence; invoices, expense documents and payments above €1,000 must state the title of the work, otherwise the cost is not eligible (art. 3(8))
  7. Submit the final application within the deadlines, after the request for final nationality. For film works no later than 180 days after confirmation of the classification or first release to the public (art. 15), for television and web works no later than 180 days after delivery of the sample copy (art. 19). Where a preliminary application was filed, the twenty-four-month limit from that application also applies, extended by twelve months for animation (art. 26(2)); for television and web animation the provision still refers to eighteen months, so it is prudent to count thirty months. Derogations may be allowed for supervening reasons promptly notified and documented (arts. 15(2) and 19(2)). The credit is used by way of set-off for 70% upon approval of the preliminary application and for the remainder upon approval of the final one; that share drops to 40% for undertakings with high production and financial capacity or at the undertaking's request, and is 100% where only the final application is filed (art. 6(5))

4. Common pitfalls to avoid

  • Costs claimed as eligible without meeting art. 3, for example payments to suppliers neither resident nor taxable in Italy
  • Incomplete or insufficient cultural eligibility documentation
  • Wrong timing, with the preliminary application filed too early before shooting, or before the request for provisional nationality
  • Natural-catastrophe insurance not attached to the application
  • Contracts with authors and performers lacking the artificial intelligence clauses
  • Invoices and payments above €1,000 without the title of the work
  • Budget not aligned with the declared type of work
  • Expenditure already used for tax credits of other States (art. 3(7)) or total public support above the ceiling (art. 4)
