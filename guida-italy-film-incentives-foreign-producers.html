Italy film incentives for foreign producers

Foreign producers looking at Italy often hear of a 40 per cent rebate. It is in fact a tax credit, and it is claimed not by the foreign producer but by the Italian company that carries out the work on commission. This guide explains what that means for the deal, and how the credit sits alongside regional film funds.

A tax credit, not a cash rebate

Article 19 of the Italian film and audiovisual law grants a tax credit to Italian production services (executive production) and post-production companies for works, or parts of works, made in Italy on commission from foreign productions. The law sets a band of 25 to 40 per cent of the expenditure incurred in Italy, and the implementing decree applies the top of the band, 40 per cent of the eligible production cost of the single work. (art. 19, Law 220/2016, and art. 3(1), Decree 329/2024)

The difference from a cash rebate is practical. The credit is not paid out. The Italian company uses it to offset its own tax and social security payments through the Revenue Agency's online services, from the fifteenth day of the month after the credit is recognised, and 70 per cent of it becomes usable once the preliminary application is approved. (arts. 6(3) and 14(1), Decree 329/2024)

Once approved on a final basis, the credit may be assigned to banking, financial and insurance intermediaries subject to prudential supervision. The Ministry's own English guide describes this as converting the credit into a direct contribution. The assignment requires an application to the Directorate General with proof of payment of a processing fee and a contract in public form, and it is available only after final approval. Counting on it to fund the shoot itself is a timing risk for any financing plan. (art. 11, Decree 329/2024)

Why the foreign producer needs an Italian partner

The foreign production company is the client, not the beneficiary. The applicant must be a limited company with its registered office in the European Economic Area, taxed in Italy by residence or through a permanent establishment to which the work is attributable, and registered under ATECO code J 59.11. A foreign producer without such a company reaches the credit only through a service agreement with one. (art. 2(1) and (3), Decree 329/2024)

The commissioning company still has a part in the file. It must attest the total production cost of the work, and the contract with the foreign production is filed with the preliminary application. The figures in that contract and in the attestation will be read against what is certified at the end, so they are worth checking against the application before signing. (art. 3(3), Decree 329/2024)

How the benefit is reflected in the price of the services is not regulated by the decree. It is a matter for the service agreement, and so is the question of who bears the loss if costs are disallowed or the credit is recognised for less than expected, a risk best allocated before signing.

Two rules bear directly on the structure of the deal. The Italian company must not hold any share of the rights in the work, save an express derogation by the Directorate General, and the work must not meet, even potentially, the requirements for Italian nationality. A project that could qualify as Italian, or in which the Italian partner is meant to share in the rights, should be assessed as a possible co-production instead. (art. 3(6), Decree 329/2024, and art. 5, Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

What the 40 per cent is calculated on

The rate applies to the eligible production cost incurred in Italy, not to the budget of the film. Above-the-line costs relating to individuals who are not tax resident in the European Economic Area attract 30 per cent instead of 40, the eligible cost must be at least 250,000 euro, and each company or group of companies may receive up to 20 million euro a year. (art. 3(1), (2), (4) and (5), Decree 329/2024)

Expenditure used to obtain another State's tax credit is not eligible in Italy. On a shoot split between countries, the allocation of each cost to one incentive or the other should be settled in the budget before filing, not reconstructed at the final account. (art. 4(5), Decree 329/2024)

The rules on eligible cost, payments, certification and forfeiture are detailed and carry real consequences for the Italian company. They are set out in our technical guide to the tax credit for foreign productions, which this guide does not repeat.

Regional film funds, and the 50 per cent ceiling

Italian regions run their own film funds, usually through their film commissions, with separate calls and separate rules. They are not part of the national credit, and a production that obtains both does so within a common ceiling. The tax credit and the other forms of public support together may not exceed 50 per cent of the cost of the work. (art. 5(5), Decree 329/2024)

The calls differ first of all in who may apply. Among the 2026 calls in the South, Apulia requires the company to have its registered office or a local unit in the region by the time of the first payment. Campania's call for production is open to companies established in the European Economic Area, including an executive producer under contract. Sicily's 2025-2027 call requires a company from outside the European Union, if selected, to sign an executive production agreement with an operator tax resident in the Union, which then becomes the beneficiary. (art. 6(4), Apulia Film Fund 2026; art. 17, Piano Cinema Campania 2026)

They also tie the grant to spending in the region. Campania asks for spending there of at least 160 per cent of the grant for feature films, and Sicily for at least 150 per cent of the grant. Campania's call expressly allows the grant to be combined with the national tax credit, including the credit for foreign productions, within the limits of EU state aid rules. Apulia's call mentions only the tax credit for Italian productions, a point worth checking with the film commission before a budget relies on both. (arts. 19 and 20, Piano Cinema Campania 2026; art. 9(8), Apulia Film Fund 2026)

Regional calls are published each year, often with short windows and changing conditions. The details above were checked on the 2026 calls on 5 November 2026 and should be checked again on the call in force before they are built into a financing plan. (Apulia Film Fund 2026; Piano Cinema Campania 2026; Sicily call 2025-2027)

When to apply

The national credit runs on annual sessions opened by directorial decree. The 2026 session closed on 31 August 2026, applications are examined in order of the declared start date of the work within the budget available, and a further session in 2026 may open only if the budget is increased. (arts. 1(2), 2(1) and 4(3), Directorial Decree 1244/2026)

As of 5 November 2026 the Directorate General had not published a decree opening the 2027 session. Since the ranking follows the start of work, the shooting calendar and the session should be planned together, and the preliminary application is subject, on pain of inadmissibility, to its own time limit, examined in the technical guide. (art. 7(1), Decree 329/2024)
